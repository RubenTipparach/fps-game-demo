# Design: NPC bodies, one animation library, ragdolls

## Context

The owner, 2026-09-27: "use blender and find opensource npc character generator, and use any
systems you can find to rig, animate, and ragdoll NPCs", and "any npc can be shot, if they can
defend themselves they will".

The hub's 22 characters today are segmented UT99-style figures (C1) with five hand-keyed clips.
The game picks a body by id, `res://models/characters/<id>.glb`: the 19 named NPCs by their
`model` in `data/npcs.json`, and civilians by a seeded pick from six models. The project already
runs Jolt Physics (`project.godot`).

Before writing this design, the pipeline was measured end to end in the cloud container
(CLAUDE.md 3). The scripts, bone maps and per-frame logs are kept in `docs/spikes/npc-pipeline/`
(reference only), and the stills are in `docs/screenshots/npc_pipeline/`. Every number below
comes from that run: 4-core Xeon at 2.1 GHz, no GPU, Blender 5.2.2, Godot 4.7.2 mono, Jolt.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every NPC body, named or civilian, generated by a committed script from a committed table,
  with only CC0 assets, byte-identical on rebuild.
- One skeleton profile and one shared animation library for every body.
- Deaths and knockouts that fall with physics and then freeze.
- A budget per body that the build enforces.

**Non-Goals:**
- Facial animation and lip sync. The game-engine rig has no jaw or eye bones.
- The combat AI that decides when to fight, flee, cower or surrender. That is
  `combat-and-enemies`, which consumes this change (its task 4.1).
- Cloth simulation. Clothes are skinned to the body.

## Decisions

### 1. Bodies from MPFB2, driven headless by a JSON table

MPFB 2.0.17 installs and runs headless in Blender 5.2.2 (`--factory-startup`, a private
`BLENDER_USER_RESOURCES`, the extension enabled per run). The build calls MPFB's own services
rather than its UI operators:
- `HumanService.deserialize_from_dict` for the body, with the `game_engine` rig, a low-poly
  proxy, and `GAMEENGINE` materials;
- `RandomizationService` for civilians, from a per-civilian seed.

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| A base human | 0.9 s |
| A finished NPC (proxy, clothes, hair, decimation, atlas, glb) | 3.1-4.4 s |

The table is `tools/blender/npcs.json`: per NPC the phenotype sliders, skin, eyes, hair, brows,
lashes, proxy and clothes, all by MPFB asset path. Civilians are `{"id", "seed"}`, and their
random range is narrowed: MPFB's default allows babies and 2 m adults, which a crowd of market
civilians shouldn't have. Adults only, height 1.55-1.95 m.

**Alternatives.** MB-Lab's output is AGPL-licensed, so it can't ship in a closed game. The
MakeHuman 1.x desktop app has no headless path worth scripting.

### 2. Assets and licences: an allowlist of pinned packs

| Pack | Pinned | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| MPFB2 2.0.17 (the tool) | SHA-256 `4f0a879d...` | code GPLv3, data CC0, output unclaimed ([LICENSE.md](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/LICENSE.md)) |
| MakeHuman system assets, `_cc0` pack | SHA-256 `b542127a...` | CC0 ([pack](https://files2.makehumancommunity.org/asset_packs/makehuman_system_assets/makehuman_system_assets_cc0.zip)) |
| Universal Animation Library, Standard | SHA-256 `18ff1a72...` | CC0 1.0 (`License.txt` in the zip; [page](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html)) |

The full hashes are in `docs/spikes/npc-pipeline/README.md`.

The 147 asset files in the CC0 pack carry no licence lines of their own, so the build can't
check licences per file. Instead:
- `tools/deps/character_packs.json` lists each allowed pack: URL, SHA-256, licence and where the
  licence is stated.
- `tools/deps/fetch_character_tools.py` downloads them outside the repository and refuses a
  mismatched hash.
- The build resolves every asset path in the table against those packs only. An asset from
  anywhere else stops the build with its path. That replaces the spec's first scenario, which
  assumed per-file licences.

The GPL tool never ships. Only its output does, and its authors claim nothing over output.

### 3. What the CC0 pack has, and the gap

| Kind | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clothes | 20 | fedora01, fedora_cocked, female casual 01-02, female elegant 01 (blouse and skirt), female sport 01, male casual 01-06 (05 is a field jacket), male elegant 01, male worksuit 01 (overalls), shoes 01-06 |
| Hair | 10 | afro01, bob01-02, braid01, long01, ponytail01, short01-04 |
| Skins | 23 | young, middle-aged and old; African, Asian and Caucasian; male and female |
| Eyebrows, eyelashes, eye colours, proxies | 12, 4, 9, 7 | |

**There are no coats, uniforms, armour or cyberpunk clothes.** The sanitation overalls map
straight onto `male_worksuit01`, and civilians can wear the casual sets. MerSec armour, the Drain
Rats' gear, the Scrap Kings' scrap and Silk's coat have to be authored. MPFB ships MakeClothes,
which fits an authored mesh to the base body as a `.mhclo`, and anything we author is ours. The
survey asks the owner how to fill the gap (H3).

### 4. The game-ready body and its budget

The raw MPFB output had 7 materials and 2048-4096 px textures. The build:
- keeps the low-poly proxy (`male1591` or `female1605`) and deletes the base mesh and helpers;
- applies the delete groups, so skin hidden under clothes is removed;
- decimates only the heavy pieces: shoes to 0.35-0.4, the bob and ponytail hair to 0.45-0.6;
- bakes to 3 materials (skin, outfit, alpha-clipped hair) with four 1024 px textures.

| NPC | Triangles | Vertices | glb, PNG textures | glb, WebP textures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bouncer | 12,432 | 7,827 | 3.81 MB | 0.76 MB |
| coat_woman | 11,766 | 7,787 | 4.11 MB | 0.81 MB |
| old_civilian | 14,774 | 8,572 | 4.08 MB | 0.81 MB |
| random_civilian | 11,596 | 7,502 | 3.44 MB | 0.70 MB |

**The budget, enforced by the build:**

| Budget | Value | Measured max |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles | 16,000 | 14,774 |
| Materials | 3 | 3 |
| Texture size | 1024 px, at most 4 textures | 1024, 4 |
| Bones | 53 | 53 |

The numbers live in `tools/blender/npcs.json` (`budget`), not in the script (CLAUDE.md 5.5).

**Textures.** WebP cuts each glb about 5x. Whether Godot 4.7 imports WebP inside a glb wasn't
checked, so task 1.4 measures it before the choice. For the 25 bodies the hub needs, that is
about 95 MB with PNG against about 19 MB with WebP.

**Frame cost is unmeasured.** A 12-15k triangle skinned body is about 10x the segmented figures.
Thirty on screen would be roughly 400k skinned triangles beside the hub's 122k static ones.
Whether Godot's import-time mesh LODs cut that for skinned bodies is part of the measurement
(task 5.2). Numbers wait for a GPU and the owner's machine (CLAUDE.md 9: never call an
unmeasured design a speedup, or a cost).

### 5. One skeleton profile, retargeted at import

The `game_engine` rig has 53 bones, all deforming, with Unreal-mannequin names (`pelvis`,
`spine_01`-`03`, `upperarm_l`, `thigh_r`, fingers `_01`-`_03`). There are no eye or jaw bones.
Stress poses bend cleanly at shoulders, elbows and knees. The skirt stretches under a raised
thigh, and the sport top creases at the shoulder: acceptable at game distance.

Godot's `BoneMapper` auto-mapper is editor-only (not in `ClassDB`), so the two bone maps are
committed `.tres` files. Each maps 53 of `SkeletonProfileHumanoid`'s 56 bones, all but
`LeftEye`, `RightEye` and `Jaw`. The import options are the bone map, renaming the skeleton to
`%GeneralSkeleton`, and the rest fixer (Overwrite Axis, Fix Silhouette). MPFB rests in an
A-pose, and both rigs come out in a T-pose. Source: [Godot, retargeting 3D
skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html).

The import options are written by a committed tool script rather than edited by hand, so a new
body gets them the same way.

### 6. One animation library: UAL Standard

The proposal said 120+ clips. **The Standard download holds 46**, measured. It imports as one
`AnimationLibrary`, and all 53 tracks resolve on all four bodies, so one library drives every
NPC. Loop clips end in `_Loop`; Godot strips the suffix and sets them looping.

| Game state | UAL clip |
|---|---|
| Idle, walk, run | `Idle`, `Walk` (`Walk_Formal` for the well dressed), `Jog_Fwd`, `Sprint` |
| Crouch | `Crouch_Idle`, `Crouch_Fwd` |
| Talk | `Idle_Talking`; sitting: `Sitting_Enter`, `Sitting_Idle`, `Sitting_Talking`, `Sitting_Exit` |
| Pistol | `Pistol_Idle`, `Pistol_Aim_Up`, `Pistol_Aim_Neutral`, `Pistol_Aim_Down`, `Pistol_Shoot`, `Pistol_Reload` |
| Melee | `Punch_Enter`, `Punch_Jab`, `Punch_Cross`, `Sword_Idle`, `Sword_Attack` |
| Hit | `Hit_Chest`, `Hit_Head` |
| Death | `Death01`, then the ragdoll |
| Use | `Interact`; dodge: `Roll` |

**Missing:** rifle holds, surrender (hands up), cower, strafing, turns in place, and more
deaths. `combat-and-enemies` needs surrender and cower for its defence table, and rifles for
MerSec. They are keyed in Blender on the UAL rig and added to the same library, so every body
gets them at once. The first two are surrender and cower.

### 7. Ragdolls on Jolt

Each NPC scene carries a `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` with 20 capsules, 80.5 kg in all:
- the joints are cones, except the knees and elbows, which are hinges limited to -140 to
  0 degrees;
- every bone from the hips out to the limbs is simulated, with no gaps in the chain;
- the joints are built at rest;
- collision exceptions cover parent, sibling and grandparent pairs. A joint doesn't exclude its
  own pair, and without that the spine opened an 8.5 cm gap.

The test drops each body, standing, off a 0.45 m step at 60 Hz:

| Engine | Peak speed | Joint gap, max | Speed at 3 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jolt, 60 Hz (4 bodies) | 6.9-7.4 m/s | 3.1-5.2 cm | 0.06-0.12 m/s |
| Jolt, 120 Hz | 7.7 m/s | 3.1 cm | 0.06 m/s |
| GodotPhysics3D, 60 Hz | 2,090 m/s | 42.7 m | 414 m/s |
| GodotPhysics3D, 120 Hz | 3,077 m/s | 53.9 m | 1,445 m/s |

GodotPhysics3D exploded in every setup. Jolt fell cleanly every time: knees bent 67-151 degrees
the right way, at most 11 degrees past the limit. The project already uses Jolt, so nothing
changes there.

**Settling.** At 3 s every Jolt body is below 0.12 m/s, but none was reliably asleep. One body
drifted back up to 0.64 m/s by 5 s. So a ragdoll simulates for `ragdoll_settle_s` (3.0 s) and
is then frozen in its pose. Waiting for sleep would leave some bodies twitching.
`ragdoll_settle_s` lives in data. The spec's requirement already says "comes to rest and stop
simulating within 3 s"; this is how.

### 8. Reproducibility

Running the build twice gives byte-identical glbs and texture atlases, even with a random
`PYTHONHASHSEED`. The `.blend` files differ between runs (Blender stamps them), which is why the
glb is the checked artifact, not the `.blend`. Godot's import wasn't checked for determinism;
it's a build product anyway (CLAUDE.md 6.1).

CLAUDE.md 6.1 wants the editable `.blend` committed beside each generated model. At 12.5 MB each,
25 bodies would be about 310 MB. The table is the true source, since the `.blend` is rebuilt
from it byte for byte except for Blender's stamp. This change proposes an exception for
generated characters: commit the table and the glb, not the `.blend`. It is listed for CLAUDE.md
section 13 once the owner agrees (survey H1).

### 9. Replacing the segmented characters

The generated glbs keep the ids the game already loads (`models/characters/<id>.glb`), so
`NpcActor` finds them unchanged. It gains:
- an `AnimationTree` fed from the shared library;
- the ragdoll hand-off on death or knockout.

`tools/blender/build_characters.py` and the segmented models are deleted in the same commit that
lands the last replacement.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **The look.** MPFB bodies read as realistic people with photo-sourced skin, unlike the UT99
  figures of C1 and next to the hub's Material Maker surfaces. The first built task puts three
  in the hub and captures them for the owner (survey H2).
- **The wardrobe.** Without authored faction outfits, MerSec and the gangs can't be told apart
  by silhouette, and the disguise system depends on outfits reading at a glance (H3).
- **Faces.** They are static. Dialog is letterboxed at conversation distance, so a frozen face
  shows. MPFB's larger rigs have face bones; they weren't measured.
- **Frame cost** is unmeasured (decision 4).
- **GPL tool, CC0 output.** The build runs a GPLv3 tool, but only its output ships. The fetch
  script keeps the tool out of the repository.

## Migration Plan

1. Tooling: fetch, table, build, budget check, bone maps, import options. The same four test
   bodies, now in the repository's tools.
2. Three hub NPCs (Tank, Nguyen, a civilian) in the hub, captured (H2).
3. The shared library and the `AnimationTree` on `NpcActor`, then the ragdoll.
4. The rest of the named NPCs and the civilian seeds. Authored outfits as H3 decides.
5. Retire the segmented characters.

Rollback: the segmented glbs stay in place until step 5, and the ids are the same.
